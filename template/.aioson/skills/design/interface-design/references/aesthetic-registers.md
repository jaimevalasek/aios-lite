# Aesthetic Registers — Interface Design

Load in **origination mode only**, when no identity record, prototype, or established component language resolves. That is the cold start — the exact moment a generative system reverts to the statistical average, because "clean modern SaaS" is what the average looks like.

`design-directions.md` answers *what the product needs* (density, warmth, trust, dark platform). This file answers *what the product sounds like*. Pick one register **and** one direction: the register sets posture, the direction sets the token math. Never mix two registers — that is style soup with extra steps.

A register is not a theme and never overrides accessibility, contrast, state coverage, or a project rule. It is the input that keeps the first decision specific instead of default.

---

## Technical

*For: developer tools, infrastructure, logistics, anything where the data is the point.*

- **Typography** — one grotesque or neo-grotesque for UI, monospace carrying real weight (labels, IDs, units, not just code). Tight, even tracking. Small sizes trusted.
- **Color** — near-neutral ground, one functional accent, semantic colors that mean status and nothing else.
- **Space** — dense and even. The grid is visible in the alignment, not in decoration.
- **Depth** — borders and rules only. No shadow.
- **Texture** — data itself: tables, sparklines, counts, timestamps, units.
- **Motion** — state feedback only, under 150ms.
- **Failure mode** — density without hierarchy. Everything the same weight is unreadable, not technical.

## Quiet

*For: premium services, portfolios, single-decision landings, products whose value is calm.*

- **Typography** — one family, wide size range, display sizes actually large. Generous leading. Line length short.
- **Color** — near-monochrome, one restrained accent, ground doing most of the work.
- **Space** — negative space as the primary element. Few objects per viewport, each with room.
- **Depth** — none or a single soft ground gradient.
- **Texture** — atmosphere, not ornament: one photograph, one gradient field, one horizon.
- **Motion** — slow, long, few. Reveal rather than animate.
- **Failure mode** — emptiness with nothing to look at. Quiet needs one thing worth the silence.

## Editorial

*For: content products, documentation, publishing, research, anything read at length.*

- **Typography** — a real typographic hierarchy: display serif or high-contrast sans for headings, a comfortable reading face for body, deliberate measure.
- **Color** — paper ground, ink foreground, accent reserved for links and marks.
- **Space** — asymmetric columns, hanging elements, generous margins. Layout follows reading order.
- **Depth** — rules, not boxes.
- **Texture** — pull quotes, marginalia, captions, figure numbers.
- **Motion** — near zero. Scroll is the interaction.
- **Failure mode** — a blog template. Editorial means composed, not centered.

## Material

*For: craft brands, food, physical goods, tools with a tactile identity.*

- **Typography** — a family with personality in the display size; body stays plain.
- **Color** — warm or earthy ground, pigment-like accents rather than screen primaries.
- **Space** — irregular, hand-placed feel with a strict grid underneath.
- **Depth** — one strategy, chosen for material: soft shadow for stacked paper, borders for panels, never both.
- **Texture** — grain, fibre, print artifacts at low opacity. Static, never animated.
- **Motion** — physical easing, short travel.
- **Failure mode** — skeuomorphism. The texture is a surface, not a costume.

## Constructed

*For: products that want to look deliberate and a little difficult — creative tools, agencies, launches.*

- **Typography** — strong contrast between display and UI: an unusual display face against a plain interface face. Type is the composition.
- **Color** — restricted palette with one uncomfortable choice, used consistently.
- **Space** — asymmetry, deliberate overlap, off-axis alignment against a visible grid.
- **Depth** — flat, with layering by overlap and scale rather than shadow.
- **Texture** — halftone, dither, pattern fills, rules.
- **Motion** — sharp, mechanical, short.
- **Failure mode** — novelty without usability. The controls stay obvious even when the composition is not.

---

## Choosing

Answer three questions before committing:

1. **What is the surface for** — inspecting data, making one decision, reading, browsing, or being convinced?
2. **What does the domain sound like** — precise, calm, considered, tactile, deliberate?
3. **What is the one signature move** — the composition decision a returning user would recognize in a screenshot with the logo removed?

If the three answers do not converge on one register, the surface probably needs the plainest option that serves the task. A conventional register applied with specificity beats an expressive one applied by default.

## Recording it

The chosen register, its anti-goals, and the signature move belong in the prototype manifest `## Visual direction` and, when the decisions are project-wide, in the identity record. A register chosen and never written down is re-decided by the next agent, which is how a product ends up with three visual systems.
